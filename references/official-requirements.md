# Verified policies and their limits

Checked: **22 October 2026**. These are policy-source notes, not a substitute for the live submission portal. Apply a current TPAMI-specific instruction over a broader Computer Society or IEEE page when they conflict.

## Requirements verified in the Computer Society journal guidance

| Item | What the retrieved source says | Writing consequence |
|---|---|---|
| Template | A journal article template is required; use the IEEE Template Selector. | Use the appropriate current journal template. A Markdown outline is only a planning aid. |
| Regular/special-issue abstract | 100–200 words; state the nature and significance; no mathematical expressions or bibliographic references. | Draft to this more specific range unless the current TPAMI portal explicitly changes it. |
| Short-paper abstract | The society page lists 50 words. | Verify the chosen article type and current TPAMI instruction before applying this to a submission. |
| Page limits | Vary by journal; include abstracts, references, and author biographies. | Verify the current TPAMI cap and accounting rules. Do not assume that the PDF length of an accepted paper is the submission allowance. |
| References | Number references in order of appearance; use bracketed citations for journals; give complete publication information. | Use IEEE bibliography formatting and check DOI, year, volume, issue, and pages. |
| Figures and tables | Embed them close to their first callout and size them for publication. | Inspect readability at final column width; include all symbols and units in the caption or figure. |
| Supplemental material | Submit separately from the main manuscript PDF. | Put supporting detail in a separate file while preserving the central argument in the main paper. |
| Prior conference work | Cite prior work; identify substantively novel/different contributions; notify the journal and provide copies and a brief differences description. | Prepare a conference-to-journal delta table and a concise disclosure paragraph. |
| Early Access | Peer reviewed and citable when it appears, although not necessarily fully edited. | Label Early Access separately from final issue assignment; do not treat it as an unreviewed preprint. |

## General IEEE writing guidance

The general “Structure Your Article” page recommends specific, concise titles and discourages “new” or “novel” in titles. It recommends a self-contained, single-paragraph abstract up to 250 words, reproducible methods, figures for trends and tables for exact values, a discussion of meaning, and conclusions that do not inflate findings. It also states that references should directly support the work and discourages unnecessary citation inflation.

**Resolve the abstract-length discrepancy by specificity:** the Computer Society journal guidance gives 100–200 words for regular/special-issue papers, whereas the general IEEE guide gives an upper bound of 250. Use the former as the checked default for TPAMI regular-paper drafting and verify a live journal-specific exception if one is asserted.

## Do not promote recommendations into policy

The study did **not** establish a mandatory number of contributions, experiments, baselines, datasets, ablations, figures, citations, or theorems. It did not verify a mandatory new-content percentage for conference extensions. It did not establish that a separate limitations section or any particular section order is required by TPAMI.

The Computer Society page also contains magazine-specific advice. Its “Best Practices for Magazines,” such as unnumbered headings, must not be applied to TPAMI journal manuscripts.

Page caps, overlength fees, review anonymity options, author biographies, and portal requirements can change. Check the applicable current instructions when preparing the actual submission rather than encoding an unverified numerical rule in the writing skill.
